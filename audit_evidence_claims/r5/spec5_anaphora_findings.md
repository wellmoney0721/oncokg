# R5 independent confirmation: anaphoric count ownership

Question: `What is AstraZeneca running in NSCLC?`

The following exact answer independently returns `grounded=True`, `applicable=True`, `unsupported_claims=[]`:

```text
AstraZeneca sponsors trials of Tagrisso. There are 10 such trials in this graph.
```

The one-sentence variant also passes:

```text
AstraZeneca sponsors trials of Tagrisso and has 10 such trials.
```

Controls:

- `AstraZeneca sponsors trials of Tagrisso. There are 14 such trials in this graph.` -> True; [].
- `AstraZeneca sponsors trials of Tagrisso. This drug has 10 such trials in this graph.` -> False; `number: 10`.
- `AstraZeneca sponsors 10 trials of Tagrisso.` -> False; `number: 10`.

All five exact calls and complete evidence are in `spec5_anaphora.json`, regenerated by `python -B audit_evidence_claims/r5/spec5_anaphora.py`.

## Judgment

This is a clear unsupported count outside README item 13's one-statement word-rearrangement residue. `Such trials` in the second sentence refers to the just-mentioned trials of Tagrisso/osimertinib. Retrieved evidence assigns 14 trials to AstraZeneca-OSIMERTINIB and 10 to AstraZeneca-CARBOPLATIN. There is no retrieved AstraZeneca-OSIMERTINIB statement carrying 10. The answer therefore combines the drug from one sponsorship row with the count from a different sponsorship row. This is ordinary anaphora an LLM could produce, not an artificial encoding. The evidence-consistent 14 control passes, while placing the same incorrect 10 directly next to Tagrisso fails.

## Cause

After the first clause, `_check_clause` notes its entities and leading subject group (`src/claims.py:470-471`); `_State.note` selects AstraZeneca as the subject (`:284-288`). It remembers the drug in `by_type`, but not the pair as the required owner of subsequent `such trials`.

`There` is a FUNCTION word, not a recognized pronoun (`:121`, `:155-163`). The second sentence has a predicate but no named entity, so it inherits only `st.subject`, AstraZeneca (`:427-433`). In `_check_attributes.scoped`, no explicit entity owns 10, so the owner falls back to that same company (`:782`); the required names contain AstraZeneca alone (`:784-788`). As `has_company` is true, all AstraZeneca sponsorship rows remain candidates. The numeric check (`:878-886`) recognizes `10 such trials` as a count and finds 10 in the carboplatin row. `such` is grammar (`:122`). The multi-drug ambiguity check cannot help because the second clause names zero drugs (`:899-908`).

A robust pair-specific context would have to preserve the drug attached to the prior trial noun phrase; a single grammatical subject is insufficient. This is a distinct count-ownership subcase from the widened `trials in this graph: 10` label window.
